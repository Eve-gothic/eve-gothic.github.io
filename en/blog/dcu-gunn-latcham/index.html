The DCU without Gunn — what we actually know about the big shake-up at DC Studios

James Gunn and Peter Safran are handing over DC Studios, and from December 1 Marvel veteran Jeremy Latcham is in charge. I separated what's official from what anonymous sources are saying.

On Monday, November 9, Skydance announced something that in comic-book-movie land sounds like Krypton exploding: James Gunn and Peter Safran are no longer running DC Studios. From December 1, the new president and chief creative officer is Jeremy Latcham, a Marvel Studios veteran. I've spent the last few days reading everything I could find, and I decided to tidy it up. Stories like this are where fact and gossip blur most easily, and I like to know what I'm believing.

What's official

Skydance's announcement says Gunn and Safran are "stepping away" from their leadership roles after four years and returning full time to creative work. Latcham will run DC's film, TV and animation slate, reporting to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, who co-chair Skydance's film group. That's a detail Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter both flagged: the previous duo answered directly to the head of the whole company, first David Zaslav, then David Ellison. The new DC boss has one more floor above him.

What's still on the calendar? Man of Tomorrow, Gunn's second Superman film, is in post-production and hits theaters on July 9, 2027. Gunn and Safran are still producing it, along with the HBO Max series The People v. Gorilla Grodd, starring Jimmy Olsen, which has already wrapped filming.

Money, the real protagonist

Variety says it plainly: according to sources, the pair "spent big" and lost too much. Superman made more than $618 million worldwide, which Skydance itself highlights. But July's Supergirl, per Variety, took $126 million against a $170 million budget. October's body-horror Clayface earned over $109 million on a much smaller budget, yet Variety's sources say it won't break even without downstream revenue. And then there's Lanterns: good reviews, strong viewership, and still no official second season, as Variety and TV Insider both note.

How it went down

This is where anonymous sources come in, so I'm treading carefully. The Hollywood Reporter says that on Friday, November 6, Ellison and Ynon Kreiz came to Gunn and Safran's offices and told them. TheWrap reports that talks with Latcham had started more than a month earlier. And here's the irony THR points out: at Marvel, it was Latcham who pushed to hire Gunn for Guardians of the Galaxy. The man who once opened the door for Gunn is now sitting at his desk.

Gunn himself wrote that the job had been "trying, to say the least," that he disliked its corporate side, and that his ADHD struggled with splitting time between projects he loves and ones he loves less. I respect that honesty. People in that chair rarely admit they're simply relieved.

Where now

According to TheWrap, Ellison expects Latcham to get the Justice League back on screen as soon as possible: Wonder Woman, Green Lantern, The Flash, the biggest names. Lesser-known heroes move down the list, and the studio is expected to be far quieter on social media. Neither Skydance nor DC has confirmed any of this. Nobody knows yet whether the current universe survives or we get yet another reboot.

My take? I have a soft spot for weirdos. Clayface, Polka Dot Man and the whole gallery of misfits are the essence of comics to me, a medium that has always been home for people who didn't fit anywhere else. I get the "icons first" logic, but Supergirl being called "modestly known" is funnier than some of the movies. I also know who'll clap the loudest: the guys for whom every heroine and every outsider is "an agenda." Relax, boys. Wonder Woman is a woman too.

Latcham starts on December 1. I'm rooting for the films, not the corporate chess. And for the new DCU to keep some room for its monsters.
