Some releases you wait for, and some you have to make room for. Rockstar confirmed that pre-load for *Grand Theft Auto VI* starts on Tuesday, November 10, at midnight local time on PlayStation 5, and at 12 a.m. GMT on Xbox Series X|S. Launch day is November 19. The file can take up to 212 GB, depending on the device. Two hundred and twelve. My game library just started to shake.

## The box that is a code

The regular shelf edition no longer contains a disc. According to The Verge and PlayStation, physical versions are "code-in-box": a download code inside, not a disc. Retailers should get them around November 12 so buyers can pre-load too. The price, per The Verge, is $79.99. I like things I can hold, so it stings a little. Then again, 212 GB on discs would be a stack of them, so I get the logic. Just don't call it a "collector's box".

## Jason, Lucia and an evening with no plans

PlayStation's own guide talks about Jason and Lucia's story, and that's the more interesting part for me than the gigabytes. A duo of leads, with a woman who isn't a side dish to the plot, is a good sign. Add Vice City and the state of Leonida, a parody of America I always enjoy more as a spectator than as a resident. Let me be clear: it's a game about crime and violence, and I'm a pacifist who adores fictional chaos. Those don't cancel each other out. Virtual car theft hurts nobody, and moral panic about games is older than Rockstar.

## The freebie

Rockstar says it will release an official cover art poster and map on launch day, free to download or as a double-sided print for $0.99 from the Rockstar Store. A map on my wall? Obviously. Small thing, very fan.

## My plan for tomorrow

Three tips, because I'd rather be helpful than just ironic:

1. Check your order history: pre-load is for people who bought the game ahead of time.
2. Turn on auto-download and rest-mode internet on PS5, like PlayStation recommends.
3. Make room *now*, not at 11:58 p.m. Delete what you haven't played in a year. You won't play it.

One more cultural note. The game is huge, expensive, and even its file size is news. I'm glad that in 2026 people can still get excited about a shared event. I'll also be watching who explains to us again that games corrupt the youth, and who simply hits download. I'm hitting download.

Good night, and may you have two hundred and twelve gigabytes free.
