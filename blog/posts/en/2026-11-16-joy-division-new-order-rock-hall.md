Some bands made me who I am. I don't just mean the patched jacket or the black bob, although those too. I mean that whole dark, gorgeous world where a guitar sounds like rain on concrete and the bass sings instead of the singer. On Saturday, 14 November, Joy Division and New Order were inducted together into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. It was one of the most beautiful and saddest ceremonies I've read about in a long time.

## What happened

The ceremony took place at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles. The two bands went in as a single entry, which makes sense: after Ian Curtis died in 1980, the surviving members of Joy Division formed New Order. Pitchfork and Stereogum point out that this package was already nominated in 2023 and 2025. Third time's the charm.

The induction speech came from Trent Reznor of Nine Inch Nails. He called them "two of the most influential groups of the last 50 years" and said there are only two types of people: those who already know how influential Joy Division and New Order are, and those yet to realise how different U2, Radiohead, the Cure, Nirvana or Depeche Mode would sound without them. You couldn't pick a better host. In 1994 Reznor covered "Dead Souls" for the soundtrack of *The Crow*, the film that raised half of my generation of goths.

## One bass on stage

Now the sadder part. Of the whole line-up, only bassist Peter Hook came. Hook left New Order in 2007 and then spent years fighting his former bandmates in court over royalties. None of the others attended, so there was no reunion.

Instead, Hook took the stage with Deftones. Chino Moreno sang "Love Will Tear Us Apart", and then CHVRCHES' Lauren Mayberry took the mic for "Bizarre Love Triangle". According to Stereogum, Reznor, Deftones and Mayberry hadn't been announced in advance. Imagine sitting in that room and suddenly hearing the first notes of that bassline.

In his speech Hook said: "I'm on my own, but I'm not alone." He thanked the fans, saying the award was for them, and closed with: "Music is eternal, fans are eternal, and we are eternally grateful." He didn't name his former bandmates. The only one he remembered was Ian Curtis.

## An irony nobody would dare to write

Joy Division's most famous song is about love tearing us apart. And there it was: at the biggest night in the band's history, the song played and the band wasn't there. If someone pitched that script, I'd call it too on the nose.

I won't pretend to know who's right in this feud. I don't know these people, I know their records. What I do know is that grief for a lost friend and years of shared work can harden into a wall no trophy can break. I have a lot of tenderness for that. I believe in love that doesn't need to lock anything up, but I also know not every break can be glued back together. Sometimes the best you can do is keep playing. Each in your own way.

## A gothic footnote

The same Hall of Fame class includes Oasis, Iron Maiden, Wu-Tang Clan, Sade, Billy Idol, Phil Collins and Luther Vandross. AP reports that several stars didn't show up at all, so a lonely Hook wasn't the exception. But only his story sounded so much like his own songs. If you weren't in Los Angeles, the ceremony airs on 8 December on ABC, then streams on Disney+ and Hulu.

Meanwhile I'm putting on *Unknown Pleasures*, then "Blue Monday", and dancing alone in the kitchen. On my own, but not alone.
